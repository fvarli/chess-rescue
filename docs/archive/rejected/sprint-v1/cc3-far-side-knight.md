## Rejection record

- **Decision.** REJECTED.
- **Phase.** Phase 2 (memo-level, lead-directed: *çok agresif ol*).
- **Reviewer.** Lead.
- **Check that failed.** R0 — *instinct vs. memory*.
- **Rationale.** CC3 fails R0 catastrophically. The position shares with P1: the rescue square (f6), the rescue piece (knight), the mate-in-one threat shape (Qxg2#). A trained P1-veteran would IMMEDIATELY recognize the position as "the P1 pattern" and play Nf6+ from position memory, not from motif instinct on fresh substrate. The only variation is the knight's starting square — a single coordinate difference that doesn't reset the instinct-vs-memory boundary.

  Additionally, A4 ("the breakaway") ALSO has its knight start on d5 and rescue at f6. So CC3 is visually nearly indistinguishable from A4 — the only differences are the surrounding piece configuration (which affects the path but not the recognizable shape of the rescue).
- **Future-sprint guidance.** A future counter-check candidate must distinguish along *multiple* axes from the existing canon, not just one. Acceptable axes of distinction:
  - Different rescue piece (rook, bishop — CC2 demonstrates this)
  - Different rescue square (anything other than f6/g6)
  - Different threat shape (not Qxg2#)
  - Different bK location (not g8)
  - Different rescue mechanism (capture vs. leap vs. block)

  At least 2-3 of these axes must differ for the candidate to feel fresh to a trained veteran.
