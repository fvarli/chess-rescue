## Original memo summary

**Motif.** `blockFile`

**Proposed rescue.** wR slides from a4 along the 4th rank to g4, blocking a black slider's threat down the g-file. (Or a knight to g5 — the memo's title.)

**Position concept.** wK g1; black slider on g8 with g-file open between g2 and g8; white piece interposes on a g-rank-4 (or g-rank-5) square.

**Author's self-rating.** Weak. Concern: too close to P3.

## Rejection record

- **Decision.** REJECTED.
- **Phase.** Phase 2 (memo-level, lead-directed: *çok agresif ol*).
- **Reviewer.** Lead.
- **Check that failed.** R0 — *instinct vs. memory* with P3 as the reference.
- **Rationale.** BF1 substantially overlaps with P3 ("block the file"):
  - Same threat shape: a black slider attacks wK-g1 down an open file
  - Same rescue mechanism: white piece interposes on the file at a middle rank
  - Same kingside-shelter atmospheric pattern

  Even with altered specifics (different blocking piece, different blocking rank), the **instinct exercise is identical**: "the file is open and the king is in danger; interpose on the file." A trained P3-veteran solves BF1 from position memory of P3, not from motif instinct on fresh substrate.

  Block-the-file is a fundamentally narrow motif in chess — there are only so many ways to interpose on a vertical lane. New positions within this motif tend to read as P3-variants regardless of cosmetic differences.
- **Future-sprint guidance.** Future `blockFile` candidates should either:
  1. Move the wK off g1 entirely (different king position changes the file dynamics)
  2. Use a **different file** for the open lane (e.g., the c-file or e-file) — the player's instinct trained on "the g-file is the threat axis" will read other files freshly
  3. Combine block-the-file with another tactic (e.g., a block that also forks a black piece, or a block that pins)

  The block-the-file motif has natural ceiling under R0; expect future sprints to produce 0-1 new `blockFile` positions max.
